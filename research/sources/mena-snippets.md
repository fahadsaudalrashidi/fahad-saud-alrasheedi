# Saudi Arabia & MENA digital-service / AI adoption

Research date: 2026-10-27. These notes distinguish official targets and measured
indicators from vendor-reported claims. Source tiers: **Tier 1** = official
government/primary source; **Tier 2** = independent or established reporting;
**Tier 3** = vendor or vendor-sponsored claim.

## Saudi public-sector digital service context

### 1. Saudi Press Agency / Digital Government Authority — Tier 1
**Published:** 2025-07-20. [Source](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2363772)

> “The Digital Transformation Index ... rising from 69.39% in 2021 to 87.14% in
> 2024.”
>
> “The Digital Experience Index ... increasing to 85.04% in 2024” from 77.26%.
>
> “The Emerging Technologies Index ... rising from 60.35% in 2023 to 70.70% in
> 2024.” The Digital Content Index recorded 71.40% since its 2024 launch.

These are distinct DGA measures, not service adoption or AI-usage rates.

### 2. Saudi Vision 2030, National Transformation Program — Tier 1
**Page accessed:** 2026-10-27; target year is 2025. [Source](https://www.vision2030.gov.sa/en/explore/programs/national-transformation-program)

> “2025 Targets”: “19.2%” of GDP from the digital economy; “92%” digital
> transformation maturity for key government services.

These are stated targets, not verified achieved outcomes. The NTP also names
“Develop the e-Government,” improve government employee productivity, and
strengthen citizen/business communication among its objectives.

### 3. Saudi Press Agency — Tier 1
**Published:** 2025-11-06. [Source](https://www.spa.gov.sa/N2437761)

> The Digital Government Authority’s 2025 Digital Transformation Measurement
> index reached 88.30%, compared with 87.14% in 2024; 26 government entities
> advanced to the “innovation” stage.

This is the 2025 result for the Digital Transformation Measurement index; do not
conflate it with the Digital Experience Index (85.04% for 2024).

## National AI strategy / government adoption

### 4. Saudi Data & AI Authority (SDAIA), National Strategy for Data & AI — Tier 1
**Strategy approval:** 2020-07-17; official strategy page accessed 2026-10-27.
[Source](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/SdaiaStrategies/Pages/NationalStrategyForDataandAI.aspx)

> The page says the strategy was approved July 17, 2020. Its approach assigns
> 2021 to “National Enabler,” 2025 to “Specialist,” and 2030 to “Industry
> Leader.” Government is a priority sector: “Adapting Data & AI in government
> with the purpose of creating a smarter as well as more effectual public
> sector.”

The page lists national ambitions/targets of top 15 in AI, 20,000+ data and AI
specialists, approximately SAR 75 billion investment, top 10 in open data,
top 20 in scientific contribution, and 300+ startups. They are strategy
targets, not independently verified outcomes or evidence of agency-level
deployments. SDAIA describes a coordinating/support role with government entities.

### 5. Saudi Press Agency / SDAIA — Tier 1
**Published:** search result page carried an official SDAIA government-adoption
announcement dated 2026 (Hijri date shown; Gregorian date not confirmed in the
result). [Source](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2537131)

> SDAIA says it seeks to build national enablers for AI adoption across
> government entities, develop regulatory frameworks, strengthen governance,
> enable optimal data use, and use AI to improve efficiency, service quality,
> transaction completion, and beneficiary experience.

This is a statement of government program intent, not a quantified outcome.
Confirm publication date and any named deployed use cases before using in
time-sensitive collateral.

## MENA enterprise CX / contact-center evidence

### 6. Lucidya, company press release distributed by GlobeNewswire — Tier 3
**Published:** 2025-06-02. [Source](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/06/02/3091527/0/en/One-in-Every-Four-New-Enterprise-Clients-in-H1-2025-Switched-From-Global-CXM-Vendors-to-Lucidya.html)

> Lucidya reports that “one in every four new enterprise clients in H1 2025”
> switched from global CXM vendors; it says its platform supports 15+ Arabic
> dialects and has 92% sentiment-analysis accuracy.

These are the supplier’s own customer and product claims, not an independent
regional market survey or independently audited benchmark. Avoid extrapolating
them to all MENA enterprises or to support automation generally.

### 7. Salesforce Arabic Agentforce launch (reported by Middle East AI News) — Tier 2
**Published:** 2025-11-26. [Source](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/salesforce-launches-agentforce-ai)

> The report describes Arabic-language support for Salesforce Agentforce in the
> UAE across customer service, employee productivity, and operational
> workflows, with 24/7 handling of high-volume tasks.

This is an announced product capability/market availability, not evidence of
customer adoption, Arabic quality, or Saudi availability. Verify current vendor
availability and supported dialects directly before procurement decisions.

## Executive product implications and caveats

- Strong public digital-service maturity is a credible tailwind for service
  operations, but published indices measure broad government maturity/readiness,
  not demand for a particular support platform. Frame product value around
  measurable service outcomes (resolution time, deflection with quality,
  answer grounding, beneficiary satisfaction) and baseline these with each buyer.
- Localize Arabic-first search and support beyond Modern Standard Arabic:
  evaluate Saudi dialects, spelling variation, code-switching, transliteration,
  names/places, and Arabic-English mixed knowledge against real buyer material.
  Vendor claims about dialect count/accuracy are not a substitute for the
  customer's own acceptance test.
- Government and regulated-enterprise procurement must validate data
  classification, hosting/data location, privacy/security, integration,
  auditability, accessibility, service-level terms, and vendor qualification
  with the buyer's procurement/security/legal teams. This research did not
  establish one universal hosting or procurement rule applicable to every
  public body or MENA country; do not market blanket compliance.
- MENA is not a single language, regulatory, or buyer market. Saudi product
  readiness does not demonstrate fit across UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Egypt, or other
  markets; localize content, dialects, integrations, residency controls, and
  procurement route country by country.
- Evidence gap: this source set contains no primary interviews, buyer
  procurement documents, measured contact-center outcomes, independent MENA
  enterprise AI adoption survey, or causal evidence that AI is already reducing
  support cost. Several search results were vendor-authored or announcement
  coverage; use them as signals, not representative market estimates.
